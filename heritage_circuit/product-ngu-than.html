Di Sản
Thông Tin
  • Áo ngũ thân, "the five-paneled robe," was the standard formal outer garment for both men and women in Vietnam from the 18th century onward, and the direct ancestor of the modern áo dài.
  • The five panels carried a family meaning: four main panels stood for the wearer's parents, and a fifth, smaller inner panel stood for the wearer.
  • It was traditionally worn with a standing mandarin collar, cổ đứng, closed by cloth-covered buttons running diagonally from the neck to the underarm.
  • An inner layer in a contrasting colour — like the cream underskirt shown here — was meant to show through at the hem and side slits, which is why the outer fabric was historically kept sheer or lightweight.
  • The garment narrowed into the modern two-panel áo dài in the 1930s; this reproduction restores the older, fuller five-panel silhouette.
  • The pearl hairpins and chest tassel echo the small ornaments once pinned at the collar for formal occasions, reimagined here in pearl rather than metal.
